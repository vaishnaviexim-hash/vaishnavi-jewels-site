For an engagement lehenga, both a light Polki choker and a diamond collar necklace are good choices; the right one depends on your outfit. A light Polki choker suits a rich, traditional lehenga in red, maroon, green or gold, while a diamond collar suits pastel, ivory or modern lehengas and evening functions. Either way, keep the engagement set lighter than your wedding-day set, and choose pieces you can wear again after the wedding.

## What is a semi-bridal set?

The engagement is often the first big function of the wedding season, but it is not the wedding itself. Many brides now choose a semi-bridal set: a necklace and earrings that look special and photograph well, but are lighter and simpler than the main bridal set. This keeps the wedding-day look fresh, and it gives you a set you will actually wear again at family functions, anniversaries and festivals.

A semi-bridal set usually includes:

1. One necklace: a choker, a collar or a single-layer necklace.
2. Matching earrings, often a little longer than everyday studs.
3. Optionally, a small maang tikka, a pair of kadas or a bracelet.
4. Your engagement ring, which is the real star of the day.

- The function is a traditional ring ceremony or roka with family rituals.

Uncut diamonds give a soft, warm glow that suits heavy embroidery. Keep the choker light by choosing a slimmer design with fewer layers, and pair it with Polki or pearl drop earrings. If your wedding set is Polki, choose a different shape or stone colour for the engagement so the two looks do not feel the same.

## When should I choose a diamond collar necklace?

A diamond collar is a good fit when:

- Your lehenga is pastel, ivory, silver, blush or a modern colour.
- The function is in the evening, at a banquet or a hotel, where diamonds catch the light well.
- You want a set that will also suit gowns, sarees and reception looks later.

A collar sits flat around the base of the neck, so it suits off-shoulder, boat and sweetheart necklines especially well. With a high neckline, a slimmer diamond choker or a pendant on a fine chain may be better. Keep the earrings in proportion: studs or short drops if the collar is broad, longer earrings if the collar is slim.

## How do I choose by neckline?

- **Sweetheart or deep V:** a collar or choker fills the space well; you can add a fine long chain only if the outfit is simple.
- **Boat or off-shoulder:** a collar or broad choker suits this best.
- **High neck or embroidered collar:** skip the necklace and wear statement earrings, or choose a very slim choker that sits above the work.
- **Dupatta draped over one shoulder:** choose a necklace that stays visible on the open side.

## Which pieces can I wear again after the wedding?

This is often the deciding question. A diamond collar or slim diamond choker can be worn later with sarees, gowns and Indo-western outfits. A light Polki choker can be worn at festivals, family weddings and pujas. Diamond earrings from your engagement set can become everyday or office earrings. When you plan the set, ask which pieces can be separated: some designs let a pendant be removed or a layer be taken off.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should my engagement jewellery be lighter than my wedding jewellery?

Usually yes. A lighter semi-bridal set keeps the wedding-day look special and gives you pieces you can wear again later.

### Can I wear a diamond collar with a traditional red lehenga?

Yes, if the lehenga is not too heavily worked at the neckline. A diamond collar with a few coloured stones that match the lehenga can look fresh against red.
