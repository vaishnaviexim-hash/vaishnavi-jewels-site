With a double dupatta or a cape lehenga, choose a choker or short necklace that sits above the drape, and wear a longer necklace only if it can lie clear of the fabric. Earrings and a maang tikka become more important, because they frame the face where the drape cannot cover them. Planning necklace length and earring weight around the drape keeps your jewellery visible in every photograph.

## Why do double dupattas and capes hide jewellery?

A double dupatta usually means one dupatta over the head and another draped across the shoulders or pinned at the front. A cape lehenga adds a layer of fabric over the shoulders and upper arms. Both cover exactly the area where long necklaces and layered haars usually sit.

So a beautiful rani haar can end up hidden under fabric for most of the ceremony. The solution is not less jewellery, but jewellery placed where it will be seen.

## Which necklace length works best?

1. **Choker.** The safest choice. It sits at the base of the neck, above the drape, and stays visible from every angle.
2. **Collar or short necklace.** Sits on the collarbone. Works if the dupatta is pinned wide at the shoulders or the cape opens at the front.
3. **Long rani haar.** Wear it only if the shoulder dupatta is pinned to one side or the cape is open at the centre, so the necklace can fall in the gap.

If you love both a choker and a long necklace, try them with the actual draping before the wedding. Our guide to [choosing a choker or long necklace for your neckline](/blog/choker-or-long-necklace-which-suits-my-lehenga-neckline) explains how necklines affect the choice.

## Why do earrings and the maang tikka matter more?

With fabric over the head and shoulders, the face is framed by the drape. Earrings and a maang tikka sit inside that frame, so they become the jewellery people notice first.

- **Earrings:** chandbalis, jhumkas or long drops show well against a head dupatta. Ask for a sahara chain if they are heavy; it can be tucked into the hair under the dupatta.
- **Maang tikka or matha patti:** a matha patti looks especially regal with a head dupatta, and a passa on one side suits a dupatta pinned to one side.
- **Nath:** if part of your tradition, it adds another point of focus near the face.

## How do I keep the jewellery from catching on the drape?

Layers of net, organza and embroidered borders can snag jewellery. Before the day:

- Check that necklace clasps are smooth and sit flat at the back.
- Make sure earring backs and tikka hooks have no sharp edges.
- Pin dupattas so they rest on fabric, not on jewellery.
- Try a full rehearsal with the outfit, dupattas and jewellery together.

## Frequently asked questions

### Choker or long necklace: which suits my lehenga neckline?

Chokers suit high, boat and off-shoulder necklines, while sweetheart and V necklines can take a choker with a longer necklace. Match the necklace to the space the neckline and drape leave open.

### Which maang tikka or matha patti suits my face shape and hairstyle?

A single drop tikka suits most faces and a centre parting; a matha patti suits a broader forehead and a low bun; a passa suits side partings. Check the weight and hook so it stays in place.

### How do I match jewellery to a heavily embroidered lehenga?

Pick one hero piece, echo one stone colour from the embroidery and keep the rest in proportion. Skip extra layers where the work is heaviest.
