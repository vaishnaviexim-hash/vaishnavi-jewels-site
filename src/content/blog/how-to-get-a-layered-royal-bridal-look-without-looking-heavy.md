To get a layered, royal bridal look with Polki, emeralds and pearls without looking too heavy, build it in steps: a maang tikka or matha patti, one layered necklace instead of several separate ones, and kadas that match. Space the layers so each one shows, keep the colours to two or three, and ask for lighter construction so the set stays comfortable through a long day. The look should feel rich, not crowded.

## What makes a bridal look feel royal?

The heritage bridal look draws on old court jewellery: uncut diamonds set in gold, green emeralds, rows of pearls and a strong frame around the face. What makes it feel royal is not the number of pieces but their richness and the way they are arranged, with a clear centre and layers that fall in order.

When the look goes wrong, it is usually because too many unrelated pieces are added. Three different necklaces in three different styles feel heavy; one well-designed layered necklace feels grand.

## How do I build the look step by step?

1. **Start with the head.** Choose a maang tikka for a centre parting, a matha patti for a broader frame, or a passa on one side for a modern twist. This sets the tone.
2. **Add the main necklace.** A Polki choker that sits high on the neck, with one or two longer layers below it, gives the classic royal shape. If possible, choose a set where the layers are designed together.
3. **Choose earrings that connect.** Chandbalis or jhumkas with pearl or emerald drops tie the tikka and necklace together.
4. **Finish with the wrists.** One or two kadas per wrist look stronger and lighter than many thin bangles.
5. **Add a nath and haathphool only if they suit your face and the overall weight.**

## How should I combine Polki, emeralds and pearls?

- **Polki** gives the warm, uncut glow and the main shape.
- **Emeralds** bring colour; use them as drops or accents rather than covering every piece.
- **Pearls** soften the look and fill the space between layers.

A simple rule: let Polki lead, use emeralds in one or two places, and use pearls to connect. If your lehenga already has strong green or multicolour embroidery, reduce the emeralds and lean on pearls.

## How do I stop the layers from looking crowded?

- **Leave space between layers.** Each necklace should sit at a clearly different length: choker at the base of the neck, a mid-length layer on the collarbone, a long rani haar lower down.
- **Match the layers to the neckline.** A high neckline leaves room only for a choker and one long layer; a deep neckline can take three.
- **Keep the earrings in proportion.** If the necklace is very full, slightly smaller earrings keep the face clear.
- **Check from a distance.** Take a photo from a few steps away. Every layer should be visible.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I match jewellery to a heavily embroidered lehenga without overdoing it?

Pick one hero piece, usually the necklace, echo one stone colour from the embroidery, and keep the rest in proportion. Skip extra layers where the work is heaviest.

### Which maang tikka or matha patti suits my face shape and hairstyle?

A single drop tikka suits most faces and a centre parting; a matha patti suits a broader forehead and a low bun; a passa suits side partings. Check the weight and hook so it stays in place.

### What jewellery should I wear with a velvet or jewel-tone lehenga?

Warm gold, Polki and uncut diamonds, or emeralds and pearls that pick up the outfit colour, suit velvet and jewel tones. Keep one strong necklace.
