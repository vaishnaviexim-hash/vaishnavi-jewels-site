A simple plan works for most brides: wear Polki for the wedding ceremony, diamond jewellery for the reception, and lighter Polki or diamond pieces for the sangeet and mehendi. Polki's warm, uncut look suits a heavy, richly coloured bridal lehenga, while diamonds suit the lighter, more contemporary outfits most brides choose for the evening reception. Choose one hero necklace per function so every look in your photos feels different.

## Why does Polki suit the wedding ceremony?

The pheras are the most traditional moment of the wedding, and most brides wear their heaviest, most embroidered lehenga for it. Polki is made with uncut diamonds set in gold, so it has a soft, warm glow rather than a sharp flash. That warmth sits well against deep reds, maroons, rani pink and gold zari, and it looks right in a mandap lit by lamps and flowers.

A ceremony set usually means:

1. A choker or collar necklace, often paired with a longer rani haar.
2. Matching earrings, sometimes with a sahara chain for support.
3. A maang tikka or matha patti.
4. Bangles or kadas to sit with the chooda.
5. A nath, if it is part of your family's tradition.

You do not need every piece to be equally heavy. Let the necklace lead and keep the others in proportion. You can see the range in our [Polki jewellery](/polki-jewellery) collection.

## Why do diamonds work for the reception?

The reception is usually in the evening, under strong indoor lights, and many brides change into a gown, a saree-gown, a lighter lehenga or a silk saree. Diamond jewellery has a crisp, bright look that suits these cleaner lines. A diamond necklace with matching earrings, a bracelet and a ring is often all you need.

Because the outfit is lighter, the jewellery can be a little more modern: a fine diamond collar, a long pendant necklace, or statement earrings with a slim chain. ## What should I wear for the sangeet and mehendi?

These functions are about movement: dancing, sitting on the floor for mehendi, hugging relatives. Comfort matters as much as looks.

- **Mehendi:** light Polki earrings, a slim necklace or a single pendant, and nothing on the hands that will get in the way of the mehendi artist.
- **Sangeet:** statement earrings or a lighter diamond or Polki necklace. If your outfit has mirror work or sequins, keep the jewellery cleaner and more structured.
- **Haldi:** keep it minimal and avoid anything you would not want stained.

These lighter pieces are also the ones you will wear most after the wedding, so choose designs that work for family functions and festivals too.

## Can I mix Polki and diamonds in one look?

Yes, as long as one style clearly leads. A Polki choker with small diamond studs, or a diamond necklace with Polki bangles from the family, can look natural. Problems start when two heavy pieces in different styles compete, for example a large Polki necklace and big diamond chandelier earrings together.

A useful test: stand in front of a mirror and ask which single piece you notice first. If the answer is unclear, remove one.

## Frequently asked questions

### What jewellery does a bride need from the wedding day to the reception?

Most brides need a main necklace set with earrings, a maang tikka or matha patti, bangles or kadas, rings and a nath for the wedding, then a lighter necklace and earrings for the reception. Smaller sets cover the sangeet and mehendi. Listing each function first stops you buying pieces you will not wear.

### How do I match jewellery to a heavily embroidered lehenga without overdoing it?

Pick one hero piece, usually the necklace, and keep the rest in proportion. Echo one stone colour from the embroidery and keep earrings lighter if the neckline is busy. Skip extra layers where the work is heaviest.

### Which statement piece should I wear with a plain or minimal lehenga?

Choose one focal piece, such as a Polki choker or a diamond necklace, and keep everything else light. A minimal outfit lets a single piece stand out, so pick the one that suits your neckline.
