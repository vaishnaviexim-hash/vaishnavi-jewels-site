A Gujarati bride usually wears a long necklace or rani haar with a choker, a nath, a maang tikka or matha patti, earrings, and bangles alongside her chudo, with her panetar, gharchola or Banarasi saree. Polki and gold-set uncut diamonds suit the red and white of the panetar and the red, green and gold of the gharchola especially well. The exact set depends on family custom, so many brides plan it together with both families.

## What are the panetar and the gharchola?

The **panetar** is a white or off-white saree with a red border, traditionally worn by the bride for the wedding rituals. The **gharchola** is a red or deep-coloured saree with a grid of gold zari and bandhani work, often draped over the bride as part of the ceremony. Many modern Gujarati brides also wear a Banarasi silk saree or a lehenga for parts of the wedding.

Each of these has strong colour and clear borders, which guide the jewellery choice.

## Which pieces does a Gujarati bride usually wear?

1. **Choker or short necklace:** sits high on the neck and stays visible when the saree pallu is draped over the head.
2. **Long necklace or rani haar:** falls below the choker; a classic part of the bridal set.
3. **Earrings:** jhumkas, chandbalis or long drops, often with a sahara chain.
4. **Maang tikka or matha patti:** frames the face under the pallu.
5. **Nath:** a nose ring, often with a chain to the ear, worn as per family custom.
6. **Chudo and bangles:** the bridal chudo, traditionally gifted by the bride's maternal family, worn with gold bangles or kadas.
7. **Rings and haathphool:** optional, depending on the bride's taste.

Families often add heirloom pieces. A new set can be designed to sit comfortably alongside them.

## Which jewellery suits the panetar?

The panetar's white or ivory body with a red border gives a clean base. Warm gold Polki, uncut diamonds and pearls look beautiful against it. A few red or green stones can echo the border. Because the pallu is usually draped over the head, the choker, earrings and tikka are the pieces seen most, so give them priority.

## Which jewellery suits the gharchola and Banarasi saree?

The gharchola already has rich red and gold. Yellow-gold Polki with pearls and a touch of emerald green suits it well; avoid too many extra colours. With a Banarasi silk saree, let the zari border guide the metal, gold zari with yellow-gold settings, and keep the necklace length clear of the pallu.

## How do I keep heavy bridal jewellery comfortable?

Gujarati wedding rituals are long and involve sitting, standing and walking around the fire. Make sure:

- Earrings have support if they are heavy.
- The nath chain sits comfortably and does not pull.
- Necklace clasps do not catch on the zari of the saree.
- Bangles slide easily over the chudo.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I choose kadas and bangles to wear with my chudo?

Keep the kadas in proportion to the chudo and the necklace; one or two strong kadas on each wrist balance a heavy set better than many thin ones. Match the metal and stones to your necklace.

### What jewellery should the mother of the bride or groom wear?

Graceful, comfortable sets that complement the bride without competing, such as a classic diamond set or a medium Polki necklace.
