A groom looks best with two or three well-chosen pieces rather than many. A pearl or Polki mala over the sherwani, one strong ring and either a kada or small diamond studs is a complete, tasteful look. Match the metal tone to the bride's jewellery, so the couple looks connected in photographs.

## Which pieces suit a sherwani?

Here are the pieces most grooms choose from, with how each one works:

1. **A mala (long necklace).** A single or double strand of pearls, or a mala with Polki or emerald accents, sits over the sherwani and is the most traditional groom piece. Choose a length that reaches the middle of the chest so it is visible above the buttons.
2. **A ring.** A diamond band, a solitaire or a signet-style gold ring. If the engagement ring is already a solitaire, a matching band can be worn on the other hand at the wedding.
3. **A kada.** A plain or diamond-set gold kada on one wrist is a strong, simple statement, especially when the sherwani sleeve is fitted.
4. **Diamond studs or a single stud.** For grooms who wear earrings, small round or square diamond studs keep the look clean.
5. **Buttons.** Gold or diamond-set sherwani buttons are a quiet way to add detail without wearing more jewellery.

Pick two or three of these. A mala, a ring and a kada together is the fullest look most grooms need.

- **The same metal tone:** if the bride wears Polki in yellow gold, the groom's mala and kada should also be yellow gold. If she wears diamonds in white or rose gold for the reception, choose a diamond band or studs in the same tone.
- **The same stone colour:** if the bride's set has emeralds, a few emeralds in the groom's mala connect the two looks.
- **A smaller scale:** the groom's pieces should support the bride's set, not compete with it.

## What should a groom wear for each function?

- **Haldi and mehendi:** keep it light: one ring and perhaps a slim kada.
- **Sangeet:** a diamond stud or a bracelet with an Indo-western outfit or a bandhgala.
- **Wedding ceremony:** the fullest look: mala, ring and kada with the sherwani.
- **Reception:** many grooms change to a suit or tuxedo. This is where a brooch or lapel pin works best, on the lapel of the blazer, along with cufflinks and a diamond band.

We suggest keeping brooches for the blazer at the reception rather than the sherwani, so the wedding-day look stays traditional and clean.

## How do I choose the right mala length and style?

- **Sherwani with a high collar:** a mala that falls to mid-chest, clear of the collar.
- **Sherwani with a dupatta or stole:** wear the mala on the open side, or choose a shorter strand so it does not tangle in the stole.
- **Slim build:** a single strand of medium pearls.
- **Broad build:** a double strand or a mala with a Polki pendant, which suits the proportion.

A mala should be comfortable to wear for hours and secure enough for the pheras. Because we make jewellery in our own manufacturing unit, we can set the length, choose a strong clasp and match the stones to the bride's set.

## Can the groom's jewellery be worn again?

Yes, and it is worth planning for. A diamond band or solitaire ring becomes an everyday ring. A plain or lightly set kada works at festivals and family functions. Studs and cufflinks suit office and evening wear. A pearl mala can be kept for future weddings in the family. When you plan the set, choose at least one piece the groom will wear often.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most traditional jewellery for a groom?

A pearl or Polki mala over the sherwani is the most traditional piece, usually worn with a ring and sometimes a kada.

### Should the groom's jewellery match the bride's?

It should share the same metal tone and ideally one stone colour, but stay smaller in scale so the bride's set leads.
