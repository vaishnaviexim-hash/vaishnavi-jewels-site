A pastel lehenga for a day wedding looks best with lighter jewellery: open, airy diamond pieces, rose-gold or soft yellow-gold settings, pearls and gently coloured stones such as pale pink, mint or sky blue. Daylight is bright and unforgiving, so the jewellery should add a glow rather than compete with the outfit, and it should be light enough to wear comfortably from the morning pheras to the afternoon lunch. One well-chosen necklace, matching earrings and a maang tikka or haathphool are usually enough.

## Why does daylight change what jewellery works with a pastel lehenga?

Evening functions are lit by chandeliers and spotlights, which flatter heavy, dense jewellery. A day wedding happens in sunlight or under a bright mandap, where every detail of a blush, ivory, mint, lavender or powder-blue lehenga is clearly visible.

In this light, very dark or very large jewellery can look heavy against a soft colour and pull attention away from the face. Lighter pieces do the opposite. Diamonds, white Polki and pearls pick up natural light beautifully, and open settings with visible gaps let the pastel fabric show through, so the whole look stays fresh and relaxed.

A simple rule: in daylight, the jewellery should look like it belongs to the outfit, not like it was borrowed from a night reception.

## Which metal colour goes with a pastel lehenga: rose gold, yellow gold or white?

- **Rose gold** is the easiest match for blush, peach, lilac and ivory. Its warm pink tone sits close to the fabric colour, so the piece blends in and the diamonds stand out.
- **Soft yellow gold** suits mint, sage, butter yellow and champagne. It brings a traditional feel and works especially well for Polki and Jadau pieces at a morning ceremony.
- **White or platinum-toned settings** suit powder blue, grey-lilac and silver-ivory. They give a cool, clean look that photographs well in bright sun.

## Diamonds, Polki or pearls: what suits blush, mint, lavender and powder blue?

**Blush and peach.** Rose-gold diamond sets with small, closely set stones look soft and romantic. Add pearl drops to the earrings or a pearl string to the necklace if you want a more bridal feel without extra weight.

**Mint and sage green.** These shades take well to white Polki in yellow gold, or to diamonds with a touch of pale green or pink stone at the centre. Keep the design floral or leafy rather than geometric to match the colour mood.

**Lavender and lilac.** Diamonds in white or rose settings, with pearls or pale pink stones, keep the look cool and dreamy. Avoid deep purple stones, which can look too dark in daylight.

**Powder blue and ivory.** A clean diamond necklace, delicate chandbalis and a slim maang tikka are often all you need. Ivory especially suits uncut Polki, which has a warm, old-world glow that works well in sunlight. ## How do I keep bridal jewellery comfortable through a long day function?

A day wedding often runs from a morning haldi or pheras through a long lunch, with photographs in between. Comfort matters as much as looks. Before you decide, try the set on and check these points:

1. **Weight on the ears.** Chandbalis and jhumkas should feel light after five minutes, not just when first worn. Ask for a sahara chain or ear support if the earrings are long.
2. **Necklace length and movement.** A short collar or a mid-length necklace sits better than a very long rani haar when you are sitting for rituals. Check that it does not catch on embroidery or dupatta pins.
3. **Maang tikka balance.** The tikka should rest flat on the parting without pulling the hair; the chain length should be adjusted to your hairstyle, not the other way round.
4. **Bangles and haathphool.** Keep them to a few pieces that slide easily over the wrist so you can eat, hold hands during the ceremony and sign the register without fuss.
5. **Clasps and hooks.** Ask to see how each clasp closes and test it yourself. A secure, simple clasp saves a lot of worry over a long day.

## Frequently asked questions

### What jewellery suits a floral or multicolour embroidered lehenga in blush, mint or mehendi green?

Choose jewellery in one dominant tone, usually white diamonds or white Polki, so it does not fight with the many colours in the embroidery. Pick up one colour from the lehenga, such as a pale pink or green stone at the centre of the necklace, and keep everything else neutral. Pearls work well here because they match every shade in the embroidery.

### Which one statement piece should I wear with a plain or minimal organza lehenga?

A single bold necklace, either a wide diamond collar or a Polki choker, is the best statement with a plain organza lehenga. Keep the earrings small and skip the maang tikka so the necklace stays the focus. If you prefer the attention near your face instead, swap the necklace for large chandbalis and wear only a slim chain.

### How do I match jewellery to a heavily embroidered designer lehenga without overdoing it?

Let the embroidery lead and keep the jewellery to two or three pieces with clean, open settings. Match the metal to the embroidery thread, rose or yellow gold for warm zari and white settings for silver work, and avoid large coloured stones that repeat what the fabric is already doing. A necklace that sits above the embroidered neckline, rather than over it, keeps both from clashing.
