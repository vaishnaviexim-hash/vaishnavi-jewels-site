With a gold, champagne or beige lehenga, choose jewellery that creates contrast rather than more gold. Diamond pieces, or Polki set with emeralds, rubies or pearls, stand out clearly against zari and tissue fabric. Pick one hero piece, usually the necklace, and let one colour from the stones carry through your earrings and maang tikka.

## Why does gold jewellery disappear on a gold lehenga?

A gold or champagne lehenga is covered in the same warm tone that yellow gold jewellery has. When the necklace and the fabric are the same colour, the eye cannot see where one ends and the other begins, and the necklace looks flat in photographs. The fix is not to wear less jewellery, but to wear jewellery with a different colour or a different kind of light.

There are three simple ways to create that difference:

1. **White light from diamonds.** Diamonds set in white or rose gold, or a diamond-heavy design in yellow gold, reflect a cool, bright light that reads clearly against warm fabric.
2. **Colour from stones.** Deep green emeralds, rich red rubies or soft white pearls give the eye something new to look at.
3. **Texture from Polki.** Uncut diamonds have a soft, glassy glow that looks different from the metallic shine of zari, especially when the Polki is set with coloured stones or pearl drops.

- **Rich gold or antique gold:** emeralds and rubies give the strongest contrast. A Polki choker with emerald drops, or a diamond necklace with ruby accents, looks clear and festive.
- **Champagne and light gold:** pearls and soft pink or green stones keep the look gentle. A diamond necklace with pearl strands suits a day ceremony.
- **Beige, ivory-gold and nude tones:** diamonds alone often work beautifully here, because the fabric is quieter. You can also add one colour, such as a single emerald line, if you want more drama.

Pick one stone colour and repeat it. If your necklace has emeralds, let the earrings and tikka carry small emeralds too.

## How heavy should the necklace be?

That depends on how much work is on the blouse and dupatta.

- **Heavy zari blouse with a high or embroidered neckline:** choose a choker or a short collar necklace that sits just above the work, and skip a second long necklace.
- **Plainer blouse with a deep or sweetheart neckline:** you have room for a choker plus a long rani haar, as long as the two pieces share the same stones.
- **Heavy dupatta worn over the head and shoulders:** keep the necklace close to the neck so it does not hide under the drape, and let the earrings do more of the work.

## What about earrings, tikka and bangles?

- **Earrings:** with a choker, choose longer earrings or chandbalis; with a long necklace, studs or shorter drops keep the face clear.
- **Maang tikka or matha patti:** use the same stones as your necklace so the look connects from the forehead down.
- **Bangles and kadas:** a pair of diamond or Polki kadas stands out better on a gold sleeve than many thin gold bangles.
- **Rings:** one strong ring is enough; let it match the stones of the set.

## Can I wear yellow gold jewellery at all?

Yes, if the design brings contrast in some other way. Yellow gold set densely with diamonds, or a gold piece with large coloured stones or pearl strands, still stands apart from the fabric. What usually does not work is a plain, all-gold necklace with no stones on a heavily zari-worked lehenga.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which jewellery suits a gold or champagne lehenga best?

Diamonds or Polki with emerald, ruby or pearl accents suit it best, because they contrast with the warm tone of the fabric. Keep one stone colour across the necklace, earrings and tikka.

### Should I avoid yellow gold jewellery with a gold lehenga?

Not completely. Yellow gold works when it is densely set with diamonds or carries large coloured stones or pearls, so it still stands apart from the zari.
