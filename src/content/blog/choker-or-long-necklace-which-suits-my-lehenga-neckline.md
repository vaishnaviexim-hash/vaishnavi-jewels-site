A choker suits high necklines, boat necklines and closed blouses, because it sits on the skin above the fabric and does not fight with the cut. A long necklace or rani haar suits sweetheart and V necklines, because it follows the open shape of the blouse and fills the space it leaves. For a bridal lehenga, many brides wear both: a choker at the throat and a long rani haar below it, which works with almost every neckline as long as the two pieces are different lengths.

## How do I match a necklace to my lehenga neckline?

The simplest rule is that the necklace should either sit clearly above the neckline or sit clearly inside it. A necklace that lands exactly on the edge of the blouse looks crowded and keeps catching on the embroidery.

1. **Sweetheart neckline:** The curved top of the blouse leaves an open heart-shaped space. A long necklace that drops into that curve looks natural, and a choker worn higher up on the neck keeps the space clear. Both together is the classic bridal pairing.
2. **V neckline:** Choose a necklace that comes to a point or a pendant that follows the V. A long chain or a rani haar with a centre drop echoes the line of the blouse. A choker works here only if the V is deep enough to leave a gap between the two.
3. **High neckline or closed blouse:** There is no bare skin for a short necklace, so a tight choker often disappears or looks squeezed. A long necklace that sits over the fabric, or statement earrings with no necklace at all, works better. If the collar is plain and unembroidered, a wide choker worn just above it can also look striking.
4. **Boat neckline:** The wide, straight line across the collarbones calls for either a choker that sits just above it, or a long necklace that falls well below it. Avoid princess-length pieces that end right at the fabric edge.
5. **Square neckline:** A straight-edged choker or a collar mirrors the shape. A long necklace with a central pendant is also comfortable here because the square opening gives it room.

## Can I layer a choker with a long rani haar?

Yes, and for a bridal lehenga this is the most popular way to wear a neck set. The choker gives the face a frame, and the rani haar adds length and fills the bodice. The two pieces should differ in length by a clear margin, so the choker sits at the base of the throat and the rani haar reaches the chest or below.

A few points keep the layered look balanced:

- **Keep one piece dominant.** If the choker is wide and heavily set, let the rani haar be lighter, or the other way round.
- **Match the metal and stone family.** A Polki choker with a Polki rani haar reads as one set; mixing in a diamond piece can work if the gold colour matches.
- **Watch the earrings.** With two necklaces, earrings that are medium in size usually look better than very long ones, since the rani haar already carries the vertical line.
- **Check the dupatta.** A dupatta draped over both shoulders covers much of a long necklace, so some brides save the rani haar for the reception when the dupatta is pinned to one side.

Many brides also ask for the choker and the rani haar to be made as separate pieces with matching clasps, so each can be worn alone later. ## Does the embroidery on the blouse change the choice?

It does. A blouse with heavy work around the neckline is already doing the job of a choker, so a long necklace that sits below the embroidery is usually the cleaner choice. A plain blouse, on the other hand, gives a choker the space to stand out.

Look at where the embroidery is concentrated. If the work runs along the edge of the neckline, keep the necklace away from that edge. If the work is on the sleeves and skirt but the bodice is plain, the bodice is yours to fill with a choker, a rani haar or both.

A wedding day can mean eight or more hours in the same set, so weight and fit matter as much as looks. When you try a necklace at our showroom, we ask you to move your head, sit, and lift your arms, so you can tell if the choker pulls or the rani haar swings too much.

## Frequently asked questions

### What jewellery should I wear with a pastel lehenga for a day wedding?

Wear jewellery that is lighter in weight and softer in tone, such as diamond or Polki pieces set in rose or yellow gold, which sit well against pale colours in daylight. A single choker with matching earrings is usually enough; add a long necklace only if the blouse neckline is deep. Pearl drops and uncut Polki both suit pastels because they do not overpower the fabric.

### Which one statement piece should I wear with a plain or minimal organza lehenga?

Choose one piece that sits close to the face, either a wide choker or a pair of long earrings, and keep everything else simple. A plain organza blouse gives a choker the clean background it needs, so this is one of the few outfits where a choker alone can carry the whole look. If the neckline is a deep V, swap the choker for a single long necklace with a centre drop.

### How do I match jewellery to a heavily embroidered designer lehenga without overdoing it?

Let the embroidery lead and pick one necklace, not two, placed away from the heaviest work. If the neckline is embroidered, wear a long necklace that sits below it; if the neckline is plain, wear a choker and skip the rani haar. Matching the stone colour to one of the thread colours in the embroidery ties the look together without adding more pieces.
