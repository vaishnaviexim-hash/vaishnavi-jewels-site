With a plain or minimal organza lehenga, choose one focal piece, such as a Polki choker, a diamond necklace or a pair of statement earrings, and keep everything else light. A simple, airy outfit gives a single piece room to stand out, so the jewellery becomes the main detail of the look. Pick the piece that suits your neckline and the part of your face or neck you most want people to notice.

## Why does a minimal outfit need only one statement piece?

Organza is light and slightly sheer, and minimal lehengas often have little or no embroidery. That clean surface is the point of the outfit. If you add a heavy necklace, big earrings, a tikka and stacked bangles all at once, the look becomes crowded and loses the fresh, modern feel you chose the outfit for.

One strong piece, with quieter supporting pieces, keeps the balance right and photographs clearly.

## Which statement piece should I choose?

Start with the neckline.

- **Deep V or sweetheart neck:** a statement necklace leads. A Polki choker or a broad diamond collar fills the space and draws the eye to the neck and face.
- **High neck, boat neck or collar:** skip the big necklace. Let large chandbalis or long diamond earrings lead, with perhaps a slim chain.
- **Off-shoulder or wide neckline:** a diamond collar or a choker with a centre drop works well; keep earrings small.
- **Sheer dupatta over the head:** earrings and a maang tikka become the focus because they frame the face.

## How do I keep the supporting pieces quiet?

Once your focal piece is chosen, everything else should be one or two steps smaller:

1. **Statement necklace:** wear small studs or slim drops, and skip the tikka or wear a very fine one.
2. **Statement earrings:** wear a thin chain or no necklace, and keep bangles to a few slim pieces.
3. **Statement tikka or matha patti:** keep both necklace and earrings simple.

A ring and a slim bracelet suit almost every option.

## Which colours suit pastel and ivory organza?

Minimal organza lehengas often come in blush, powder blue, mint, lilac, ivory or champagne.

- **Blush, peach and lilac:** rose-gold diamond settings blend softly with these shades.
- **Mint and sage:** white Polki in yellow gold or diamonds with a touch of pale green.
- **Powder blue and ivory:** white diamonds or uncut Polki; pearls add softness.

## How do I check the quality of a statement piece?

Before you buy, ask to see:

- The jewellery grading report from IGI, EGL or TGL, which states the diamond quality and origin.
- For any solitaire, the laser-inscribed report number on the stone, which you can verify on the laboratory's website.
- The BIS HUID hallmark on the gold, which you can check in the BIS Care app.

## What is a quick test before the day?

Wear the outfit with your chosen statement piece and take a photo from a few steps away. Your face and the statement piece should be the first things you notice. If something else catches the eye first, such as a bangle or a tikka, make it smaller or remove it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Choker or long necklace: which suits my lehenga neckline?

Chokers suit high, boat and off-shoulder necklines, while sweetheart and V necklines can take a choker with a longer necklace. Match the necklace shape to the space the neckline leaves open.

### What jewellery should I wear with a pastel lehenga for a day wedding?

Lighter diamond or rose-gold pieces, pearls and soft coloured stones suit daylight. Keep it comfortable for long functions.

### How do I match jewellery to a heavily embroidered lehenga?

Pick one hero piece, echo one stone colour from the embroidery, and keep the rest in proportion. Skip extra layers where the work is heaviest.
