A floral or multicolour embroidered lehenga in blush, mint or mehendi green pairs well with Polki, uncut diamonds, pearls or a touch of emerald. Pick one hero piece, usually the necklace or the earrings, and keep the stone colours close to a single shade from the embroidery so the flowers stay the focus. White and warm-gold tones work with almost every floral palette.

## Why do floral lehengas need careful jewellery choices?

Floral embroidery brings many colours at once: pinks, greens, yellows, blues and gold thread scattered across the skirt and blouse. The beauty of the outfit is in that soft, garden-like mix. If the jewellery adds several more colours, the look becomes busy and the flowers lose their charm.

The goal is jewellery that frames the outfit rather than repeating it.

## Which jewellery suits each colour?

**Blush and rose pink.** Rose-gold diamond settings blend gently with blush. White Polki with pearl drops gives a softer, more traditional feel. If you want colour, a pale pink stone at the centre is enough.

**Mint and sage.** White Polki in yellow gold looks fresh against mint, as do diamonds with a small green accent. Keep designs floral or leafy rather than sharply geometric.

**Mehendi and olive green.** Warm gold and Polki suit these earthy greens well. Emerald drops tie the jewellery to the outfit; pearls lighten it.

**Multicolour on ivory.** Let white lead: diamonds, white Polki or pearls. Pick one colour from the embroidery, perhaps green, and repeat it once.

## Should the jewellery be floral too?

It can be, and floral motifs often look natural with floral embroidery. But keep the jewellery motif simpler than the fabric. A necklace with a few clear flower shapes works; one covered in small flowers of different colours competes with the lehenga.

Leaf, vine and paisley forms also suit floral outfits and add variety without clashing.

## Which hero piece works best?

- **Light blouse, heavy skirt:** a choker or a Polki necklace with pearl drops leads.
- **Embroidered blouse:** let earrings lead, such as chandbalis with pearls or emerald drops, with a fine chain at the neck.
- **Sheer floral dupatta over the head:** a maang tikka and earrings frame the face better than a long necklace.

Keep bangles to a few matching pieces and choose one ring.

## Is this look good for day functions?

Yes. Floral and pastel lehengas are popular for mehendi, haldi and day weddings, where sunlight shows every detail. Lighter, open designs, pearls and soft settings look best in daylight. Our guide to [jewellery for a pastel lehenga at a day wedding](/blog/what-jewellery-to-wear-with-pastel-lehenga-day-wedding) covers daytime choices in more detail.

Comfort matters at these long, active functions. Choose earrings that feel light after an hour, a necklace that does not catch on embroidery, and bangles that slide on easily.

## Frequently asked questions

### What jewellery looks best with an ivory or off-white lehenga?

Ivory is a clean base, so gold-set Polki, uncut diamonds, pearls with emeralds or a diamond choker can lead. Add one colour through the stones if you want contrast.

### What jewellery should I wear with a mirror-work or sequin lehenga for the sangeet?

When the outfit already shines, choose fewer pieces with a clean, structured shape, such as statement earrings or a defined diamond or Polki necklace.

### Which statement piece suits a plain or minimal organza lehenga?

Choose one focal piece, such as a Polki choker or diamond necklace, and keep everything else light.
