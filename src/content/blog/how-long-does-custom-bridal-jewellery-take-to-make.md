## How long does custom bridal jewellery take once the design is final?

The honest answer is: it depends on the piece, but for most bridal orders at Vaishnavi Jewels the range is 2 to 4 weeks from the day the design is agreed.

A single diamond necklace with a simple layout sits at the shorter end. A full [Jadau jewellery](/jadau-jewellery) bridal set with a choker, long necklace, earrings, maang tikka and haath phool, each piece hand-set with many stones, sits at the longer end.

The reason we can give a realistic number is that the jewellery is made in our own manufacturing unit rather than ordered from somewhere else. We see the piece at every stage, so when we say two weeks or four weeks, it is based on what is actually on the bench.

The clock starts only when the design is final. Choosing the design, the gold karat and the stones is a separate step, and it is the part that takes brides the longest. Give yourself a comfortable gap for it.

## What happens in those 2 to 4 weeks?

Here is what goes on between "yes, make it" and "it is ready":

1. **Design lock.** We confirm the sketch or reference, the gold karat, the type of stones (natural diamond, lab-grown diamond, Polki or Jadau) and the sizes for rings, bangles and chokers.
2. **Making in our own manufacturing unit.** The gold work is made, and then the stones are set. Hand-set Polki and Jadau pieces take longer than diamond pieces with machine-precise settings.
3. **Hallmarking and reports.** All our gold jewellery is BIS HUID hallmarked, and every piece comes with a jewellery grading report from IGI, EGL or TGL. If the piece has a solitaire, that stone has its own grading report and is laser-inscribed with the report number.
4. **Final check and trial.** We check the finish, the clasps and the fit. If a ring or bangle needs resizing, it is charged on actuals and done before you take the piece home.

Each of these steps needs time, which is why we do not quote "a few days" for bridal work even when the design looks simple.

## When should I place the order before my wedding?

Work backwards from the wedding date, not forwards from today.

- **Wedding day:** jewellery should already be with you, tried on with the outfit.
- **1 week before:** keep this free for last-minute resizing and for packing. Do not plan to collect a bridal set the day before.
- **2 to 4 weeks before:** this is the making window. The design must be final before it starts.
- **6 to 8 weeks before:** place the order. This gives the full making time plus a buffer for a trial and for delivery.
- **Earlier than that:** start shortlisting. Visit the showroom or ask for photos and videos of pieces on WhatsApp, decide between Polki, Jadau or diamond, and settle the budget so the design can be made to suit it.

If you are ordering a complete trousseau with several sets, or if you want pieces that match an outfit that is itself being stitched, start even earlier so the jewellery and the lehenga can be finalised together.

Outstation and NRI brides should add delivery time on top. We offer free insured [delivery across India](/delivery-across-india) to major cities, with photos and videos of the finished piece on WhatsApp and payment by NEFT or UPI, so you can approve the final piece before it ships.

## What can delay a custom bridal order?

The making time itself is predictable. What usually stretches a timeline is everything around it:

- **Design changes after work has started.** Adding a drop, changing the length of a necklace or switching stone size can mean reworking a finished section.
- **Sizes not confirmed.** A ring or bangle made to a guessed size will need resizing, which adds days at the very end when there is least time.
- **Waiting to decide between options.** Many brides keep two or three designs open. The order cannot begin until one is chosen.
- **Peak wedding months.** When many brides are ordering for the same weeks, locking your design early keeps your slot in our manufacturing unit.

The best protection is simple: share the wedding date at the first visit, decide the design in one or two sittings, and get your sizes measured at the showroom rather than estimating them.

## How do I check my custom piece when it is ready?

Before you take delivery, check three things yourself. We will show you how at our Parle Point showroom.

- **HUID hallmark.** Find the six-character HUID on the gold and enter it in the BIS Care app to confirm the hallmark details.
- **Grading report.** Read the jewellery grading report from IGI, EGL or TGL. It states the quality and whether the diamonds are natural or lab-grown. Small diamonds are described by total weight, number of pieces and quality, so you can match the report to the piece.
- **Laser inscription.** If your set includes a solitaire, we will show you the report number inscribed on the stone's girdle under a loupe, and you can verify that number on the laboratory's website.

Doing this check a week before the wedding, rather than on the day, means any question can be answered calmly. You can read more about reports and hallmarking on [our FAQ](/faq).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days before a November-December wedding should I confirm a made-to-order set?

Confirm the design at least 6 to 8 weeks before the wedding, which for a late-November or December wedding means September or early October. Peak season means our manufacturing unit is busiest in these months, so an early confirmation keeps your 2 to 4 week making window on schedule. Starting your shortlist in August gives you time to compare sets without rushing.

### How do I choose a wedding band that sits flush with my solitaire engagement ring?

Bring the engagement ring with you, or send us clear photos of it on WhatsApp, and we will shape the band to follow the solitaire's setting so the two sit together without a gap. Because the band is made in our own manufacturing unit, its curve, width and gold karat can be matched to your ring. A matched band is a custom piece, so allow the same 2 to 4 weeks for it.

### Polki, Kundan or Jadau: which lasts and can be re-worn after the wedding?

Polki (uncut diamonds set in gold) and Jadau (stones embedded in gold using the traditional jadau technique) are both gold-based and can be re-worn, re-set or passed on, while traditional Kundan is often glass-based and is mainly a style choice. If re-wearing matters, choose a [Polki jewellery](/polki-jewellery) or Jadau set where the choker and earrings also work on their own with sarees. Ask for the BIS HUID hallmark on the gold and the grading report for the Polki so the piece can be identified years later.
