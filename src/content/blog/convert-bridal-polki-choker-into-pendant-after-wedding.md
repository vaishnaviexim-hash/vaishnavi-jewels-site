## How does a convertible Polki choker come apart?

A convertible choker is built in sections instead of as one solid piece. The usual parts are:

1. **A centre piece.** This is the main motif of the choker, often the largest Polki stones. It sits on a hidden fitting, usually a small screw or a hook-and-bar, so it can be lifted off without any tools.
2. **Two side sections.** These are the Polki links on either side of the centre. They end in small hooks or loops so they can either hold the centre piece or close directly into each other.
3. **A dori or chain at the back.** The adjustable thread or chain stays as it is, so the shorter version still sits correctly on the neck.

With the centre piece removed, the side sections join to form a slimmer Polki necklace. The centre piece then goes on a plain gold chain or a Polki chain as a pendant. Some brides also ask for a loop on the centre piece so it can hang from a maang tikka hook, giving a third use.

The fittings are the most important part of this design. They must open smoothly but not slip while dancing or sitting through a long ceremony. In our unit, each fitting is tested on the piece itself before it leaves, and we show you how to open and close it at the showroom.

## What can I wear after the wedding from one choker?

This is where the design earns its place in your jewellery box. From one convertible Polki choker you can get:

- **A pendant for sarees and silk kurtas.** The centre piece on a fine gold chain works for a family puja, a Diwali evening or a cousin's sangeet.
- **A slim Polki necklace for festive wear.** The joined side sections make a light choker that sits well with an anarkali or a simple lehenga.
- **A statement piece for the next big wedding in the family.** Put it all back together and the full bridal choker is ready again.
- **Earrings or a maang tikka, if planned.** If you want more flexibility, we can design matching earrings whose drops also detach, so you have studs for daytime and long drops for evenings.

Because Polki stones are uncut natural diamonds set with gold foil at the back, they give a soft glow rather than a sharp flash. This is why a Polki pendant looks right even on an ordinary festive day, without feeling too bridal.

## Can a choker I already own be converted?

Sometimes, but it depends on how it was made. Traditional Jadau work sets the stones into gold over a lac filling, so the sections are often joined permanently and cutting them apart can damage the setting. A choker that was built as linked sections is easier to work with, since the links can be opened and new hooks added.

The honest answer is that it has to be looked at in hand. Bring the piece to our Parle Point showroom, or show it to us on a video call, and we will tell you which sections can be separated, what new fittings are needed and whether any stones will need to be reset. If the piece has sentimental value and conversion is risky, we will say so, and suggest a matching new pendant in the same style instead.

## What should I check before I confirm a convertible design?

A few practical checks make the difference between a piece you actually re-wear and one that stays in the locker:

- **Try the short version on.** Make sure the joined side sections sit evenly and do not twist at the join.
- **Try the pendant on a chain.** Check that it hangs straight and that the fitting is hidden from the front.
- **Open and close every fitting yourself.** You should be able to do it without help on the wedding morning.
- **Read the report and the HUID.** Match the number on the report to the piece, and check the HUID in the BIS Care app before you leave.

## Frequently asked questions

### Polki, Kundan or Jadau – which is worth buying for a bride if I want it to last and be re-worn?

Polki is the stone (uncut natural diamond), Kundan usually refers to glass or coloured stones set in gold foil, and Jadau is the setting technique used for both. For a piece you plan to re-wear and pass on, Polki set in hallmarked gold is the one most families choose, especially when it is designed to come apart into smaller pieces.

### Can I build my bridal set piece by piece and still have it look like one matched set?

Yes, if the pieces are made in the same unit from the same design drawings. We keep your design on file, so earrings, a maang tikka or a haath phool ordered later are made to match the original choker in stone size, gold colour and finish.

### How many days before a November–December wedding should I confirm a made-to-order set?

Custom and bridal pieces usually take 2 to 4 weeks, so confirm at least a month before the first function. For a convertible set with several fittings, add a little more time for a trial so you can practise opening and closing the pieces.
