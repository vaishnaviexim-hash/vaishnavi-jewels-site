When your sangeet or night-reception lehenga already shines with mirror work or sequins, wear fewer pieces with a clean, structured shape. Statement earrings or a single defined diamond or Polki necklace work better than many layers, because they hold their outline against a reflective outfit. Keep everything comfortable enough to dance in.

## Why does a shiny outfit need simpler jewellery?

Mirror work and sequins catch light from every angle, especially under stage lights and spotlights at a sangeet. If the jewellery also has many small, light-catching parts, the two blur together and neither stands out. In photographs, a busy necklace on a sequinned blouse can simply disappear.

Clean shapes solve this. A collar with a clear edge, earrings with a defined outline, or a necklace with one strong centre stays visible against a reflective surface.

## Which pieces work best?

**Statement earrings.** Often the best choice for a sangeet. Long diamond drops, geometric earrings or chandbalis frame the face, move nicely when you dance and do not compete with the blouse. Pair them with a fine chain or no necklace. See our [diamond earrings](/diamond-earrings) collection.

**A structured necklace.** If you prefer a necklace, choose one with a firm shape: a diamond collar, a choker with a single centre motif, or a Polki necklace with a clear outline. Wear smaller earrings with it.

**A bracelet and a ring.** A diamond bracelet or a single kada and a cocktail ring finish the look without adding weight.

Skip the maang tikka, or wear a very slim one, if your hair is open for dancing.

## How do I match metal and stones to mirror work?

- **Silver mirror work and sequins:** white diamond settings look cleanest.
- **Gold sequins or gota with mirrors:** yellow or rose-gold settings and Polki suit the warm tone.
- **Multicolour mirror work, often seen on Gujarati and Rajasthani styles:** let white diamonds or white Polki lead, and add one colour at most.

Avoid large coloured stones that repeat the outfit's bright colours; they add noise rather than contrast.

## How do I keep the look comfortable for dancing?

A sangeet means hours on your feet, spinning and moving. Check these before the night:

1. **Earring weight.** Long earrings should feel light after an hour. Ask for a sahara chain or ear support if needed.
2. **Necklace movement.** A choker or collar stays in place better than a long necklace that swings.
3. **Clasps.** Make sure nothing catches on mirror edges or sequin threads.
4. **Bracelets.** Choose ones that close securely and sit snug at the wrist.

## Does this advice also work for night receptions?

Yes. Many brides choose sequinned or crystal-embellished lehengas and gowns for the reception. The same rule applies: one strong focal point, clean outlines and fewer layers. ## What is a quick check before the sangeet?

On the day, put on the outfit and jewellery together and stand under bright light, the way the stage will be lit. Take a short video while you turn and move. If the earrings tangle in your hair, the necklace twists, or the jewellery disappears against the blouse in the video, swap one piece for something with a cleaner shape. This two-minute check saves a lot of worry once the music starts.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which diamond or Polki jewellery goes with a sequin or crystal net lehenga?

Keep the jewellery soft and clean: uncut or rose-cut diamonds, one hero necklace and slim earrings, so the fabric's shine does not compete.

### What jewellery suits a floral or multicolour embroidered lehenga?

Polki, uncut diamonds, pearls or a touch of emerald suit soft floral colours. Pick one hero piece and keep stone colours close to one shade.

### Can I wear my diamond ring every day?

Yes, a well-set diamond ring can be worn daily. Take it off for the gym, heavy housework and chemicals, and get the prongs checked once or twice a year.
