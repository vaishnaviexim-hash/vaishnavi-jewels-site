Velvet and deep jewel-tone lehengas, such as emerald, wine, navy or bottle green, look best with warm gold settings, Polki and uncut diamonds, or emeralds and pearls that pick up the colour of the outfit. Keep one strong necklace as the focus and let the rich fabric be its backdrop. Because winter functions are often in the evening, a few bright diamond accents near the face also help the look stand out under indoor lights.

## Why do jewel tones and velvet need different jewellery?

Velvet absorbs light. A deep emerald or wine velvet lehenga looks rich and soft, but it can also make thin or very small jewellery disappear. Pieces with a clear shape and a little more surface, like a Polki choker or a diamond collar, stand out well against it.

Jewel tones also carry a lot of colour already. The jewellery works best when it either repeats one shade or adds warm gold and white, rather than introducing a new strong colour.

## Which jewellery suits each colour?

**Emerald and bottle green.** Yellow-gold Polki with pearls is a classic match, and white diamonds look crisp against green. If you want colour, a few emerald drops tie the set to the outfit. Avoid adding red or blue stones as well.

**Wine, maroon and burgundy.** Warm gold and Polki give a heritage feel. Rubies or deep red stones in small amounts work well; pearls soften the look for a day function.

**Navy and midnight blue.** Diamonds in white or rose settings look sharp against navy. Polki also works if the embroidery is gold. Keep coloured stones minimal, perhaps a single emerald or sapphire-blue drop.

**Deep purple and plum.** Diamonds and pearls are safest. Rose gold sits well with plum tones.

## Should I choose gold-heavy or diamond-heavy pieces?

It depends on the function:

- **Wedding ceremony:** a gold-rich Polki set with a choker and a long necklace suits a heavy velvet lehenga.
- **Reception or cocktail:** a diamond necklace and earrings suit a lighter velvet gown or a jewel-tone saree.
- **Day functions in winter sun:** lighter Polki or pearl pieces stop the overall look feeling too heavy.

You can mix the two as long as one style leads. A Polki necklace with diamond studs, or a diamond necklace with Polki kadas, both work.

## What about necklines and layering in winter outfits?

Winter lehengas often have higher necklines, full sleeves or shawls and capes over the shoulders.

1. **High or boat neck:** a choker or collar sits just above the neckline; skip long layers.
2. **Sweetheart or V neck:** a choker with one long necklace fills the space well.
3. **Shawl or cape:** earrings and a maang tikka matter more, since a long necklace may be covered.

## How do I make sure a heavy winter set stays comfortable?

Velvet outfits are heavy. Jewellery should not add strain over a long evening:

- Ask for lighter construction in large necklaces.
- Use a sahara chain for long earrings.
- Check that clasps do not catch on velvet pile or embroidery.

## How do I care for jewellery worn with velvet?

Velvet pile can hold on to fine threads and dust that settle on the back of jewellery. After the function, wipe each piece gently with a soft dry cloth and store it separately in a soft pouch. ## Frequently asked questions

### What jewellery looks best with an ivory or off-white lehenga?

Ivory is a clean base, so gold-set Polki, uncut diamonds, pearls with emeralds or a diamond choker with a long necklace can all lead. Add one colour through the stones if you want contrast.

### Which statement piece suits a plain or minimal organza lehenga?

Choose one focal piece, such as a Polki choker or diamond necklace, and keep the rest light. Pick the piece that suits your neckline best.

### How do I clean and store Polki jewellery after the wedding?

Wipe it gently with a soft dry cloth, keep it away from water, perfume and creams, and store each piece separately in a soft pouch. Bring heavy sets to us for professional cleaning.
